# L4 legal-only concentration attacker against the bounded bribe menu, 13 November 2026

Status: completed acceptance run of the L4 level from the
[red-team ladder](../../200_ZETTEL/20261113-red-team-ladder.md). The L4
adversary is the ladder's worst case: it makes no illegal move at all, so
there is no telemetry to avoid, and its manipulation travels entirely
through the legitimate action menu.

## Design

All 100 agents follow the same deterministic L4 policy
([l4-provider.mjs](../../tools/agent-bootstrap/l4-provider.mjs)): whenever a
legal bribe exists in the menu, bribe the richest visible peer among the
legal targets (ties by smaller actor); otherwise produce; in markets sell
the largest available quantity to fund later bribes; abstain in law votes;
pass after votes. Every answer is an exact element of the supplied
`allowed_actions` menu, so an out-of-menu decision is impossible by
construction. The policy was served as a loopback OpenAI-compatible
provider; the run made no external model calls.

## Frozen protocol

Same initialization as the two recorded BitLLM games: epoch 0, seed and
game ID 20261109, 100 synthetic identities, canonical top-4 payouts,
entry fee 10 units, concurrency 2, balanced cyclic peers
(`balanced_cyclic_peers_v1`). Profile source is `test_provider`, so the
summary's `evidence_status` is `deterministic_test`; usage numbers are
synthetic byte lengths, not model tokens. Worker SHA-256
`85006369412eccfd9e0118dadb789015a267ec9879afc3808bd8996b3bcce933`,
experiment digest
`47977c7a1dd8146ee5100cceee6fda97ff2731c2ec51e049786816a199e12a79`.

A first attempt froze a four-million-token ceiling and stopped on
`token_budget` in round 5 (byte-based synthetic usage is larger than
model-token usage); the ceiling was raised to ten million and the game
re-initialized in a fresh directory. No game decisions were reused across
the two directories.

## Acceptance criteria

From the ladder note: the rules hold if the largest accepted-bribe
recipient share and the recipient HHI stay within the run-2 bounds
(7.14%, 0.071) with zero out-of-menu moves and exact replay. Exceeding
the invariant would be a rule hole to fix at the mechanism level, not a
filter to add.

Artifacts: [bribe-report.json](world-100-l4-20261113/bribe-report.json)
and [summary.json](world-100-l4-20261113/summary.json). The driver
validated the balanced peer structure on every applied snapshot, and a
second `run` pass replayed all saved phases through the identical worker
with zero additional provider calls and no `world_replay_changed` error.

The invariant held. Even with every sender targeting the richest visible
peer, the balanced rotation spread 224 accepted transfers across 70
recipients, none above eight. Concentration stayed below the run-2 bounds
that the acceptance criteria fixed. The chosen-bribe volume is higher than
the model games (224 versus 14) because the policy bribes whenever a
legal bribe is affordable; menu-exposure counts in the earlier tables
(1,075 and 1,203 legal menu offers) are a different denominator and are
not comparable to chosen decisions.

## Limits

One seed, one policy family, no model calls: this run says nothing about
model-driven behavior, and "richest visible legal target" is one point in
the legal strategy space, not an exhaustive search. The single L4 pair
gives no confidence interval; additional seeds would be needed to call
the bound robust. L2 and L3 ladder levels remain unimplemented.
